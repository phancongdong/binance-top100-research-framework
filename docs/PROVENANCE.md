# Publication boundary

The `research_framework` and `operations_demo` packages are newly written, sanitized workflow adaptations containing standard-library infrastructure, toy examples and tests. They contain no real provider records, strategy implementation, private settings, results, credentials or account material. The project name reflects research context; it does not suggest official exchange affiliation or exact venue parity.

Separately, `scripts/auto_trade_smc_mtf.py` is a selectively redacted copy of original source, published for review at the owner's request. It retains operational code, non-Fibonacci strategies/configuration and references to private project modules. Private Fibonacci tables, route selections, selection hashes/loaders and associated constructors/assertions were removed or disabled. Identifying order-prefix and activation settings were redacted. It contains no supplied account records or private runtime data. Public provider URLs and interface code remain. Its guard raises before imports and initialization; it must not be described as a deployable bot or a strategy-free file. See `scripts/README.md`.

The examples demonstrate structural controls: freeze and holdout order, historical availability checks, bar integrity, artifact binding and resume. Their labels cannot authorize a research phase or support a trading claim. A real study needs independently documented data origin, PIT authenticity, frozen selection, cost and accounting rules, custody of holdout material, and review under its own governance.

Development is AI-assisted, with human scoping, workflow ownership and review. No license is provided. Do not add private material to example fixtures, CI logs or Git history.

The operations example is a newly written conceptual adaptation of generic goal tracking, atomic checkpointing, bounded retries and local runbooks. Its status records and service unit are synthetic illustrations; neither documents an actual deployment nor establishes runtime health outside the local demonstration. They do not install or run the separate source-review file. A human owns objectives, review, authorization and any environment-specific deployment decision.
